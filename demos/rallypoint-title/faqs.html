Title insurance & 1031 exchanges

All you need to know about title insurance & 1031 exchanges.

Short, plain-English video answers from our team — covering title insurance, refinances, wire fraud, 1031 exchanges, and the closing process.

What is title insurance?

The purpose of title insurance is to protect a buyer or owner from claims against their ownership. As a buyer, you want to protect your investment.

How long does the title policy last?

The title policy lasts the duration of your home ownership. It is paid one time at closing — not a recurring fee.

How much does title insurance cost?

Title insurance premiums are regulated state by state. Typically dependent on your purchase price and the loan amount of your property.

Who orders the title search?

In New Jersey, title insurance is traditionally at the cost of the buyer, and can be chosen by the buyer or by one of the professional parties they're working with.

Owner's vs Lender's title policy

Your owner's policy covers the buyer or owner of the property against claims. The lender's title policy covers the lender's interest in the property.

Is title insurance necessary on a refinance?

Yes — title insurance is usually required by any mortgage company to close. A refinance does qualify for a lower rate.

How to prevent wire fraud on real estate transactions?

Protect your transaction and your peace of mind. Learn how to spot and prevent wire fraud before it's too late.

What is a 1031 Exchange and how does it work?

A 1031 Exchange lets you defer capital gains taxes by reinvesting proceeds into another qualifying property. With strict deadlines and IRS rules, having the right team matters.

What are the steps in a real estate closing?

The closing process involves reviewing title, resolving issues, and finalizing documents to transfer ownership. A smooth closing keeps your transaction secure.
